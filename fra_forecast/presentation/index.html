Applies the challenge's Section 3 baseline: landings need under 10 kt tailwind, departures under 15 kt, and crosswind above 40 kt closes a runway. A small tailwind is tolerated for 30 minutes; a wind you leave in place counts as persisting. Gusts are not modelled here, and this is weather suitability, not an official runway status.

It learns from its
own mistakes, every hour.

Every hour we keep what we forecast. When the airport's weather station has measured that hour, we score the forecast we actually published, then update the correction. Each forecast is only ever corrected with errors measured before it was made.

Forecast versus measured, live

Wind speed, wind direction and temperature are checked against DWD station 01420 every hour. The latest check appears here about an hour after the first live forecast is measured.

This is online learning from forecast error, not reinforcement learning: there is no reward, only the measured miss. Operating direction and Runway 18 still use the raw forecast; the learned wind is published next to it. Learning report

Three steps,
no magic.

  1. Take the weather forecast

    Every forecast starts from the ICON-EU weather model's wind for the airport.

  2. Correct the local bias

    Trained on six months of forecasts compared with what Frankfurt's wind sensor measured, the model adds the error it expects. We tried gradient boosting and a simpler linear correction; the simpler one was more accurate on unseen data, so it is the one shown here.

  3. Translate to runways

    The corrected wind is checked against the runway wind limits. A second model, trained on six months of observed operating directions, estimates 07 or 25 for every hour and flags the next likely change.
